    Notes on the figures used
    • Pick a tariff category and all charges reload. Energy charge and wheeling charge come from page 100, cross-subsidy surcharge from page 106 (its EHV and HT values differ for some categories), and the ToD rebate/charge from Table 8 (pg 76-77), all for FY 2026-27. Operating charges, transmission charge and transmission loss do not change with category.
    • Stranded energy is valued at the RE price (the conservative choice: you pay your PPA for that energy and receive nothing, because MSEDCL pays nil against the ₹2.24/unit surplus tariff). You may, at your discretion, lower the rate in the charges panel to “RE price − ₹2.24” — but that assumes MSEDCL actually pays the surplus tariff, so it is not a conservative approach.
    • Stranded generation is capped at 10% of total generation. If your plant breaches this the tool flags it — use Optimise or raise daytime consumption.
    • Banking is worked down the value ladder (para 18.21): peak surplus serves peak, then normal, then solar demand; normal surplus serves normal then solar; solar surplus can serve only solar and is otherwise stranded. Optimise sweeps plant sizes and picks the MW with the highest net saving within the 10% cap.
    • ToD rebate on solar hours is taken as 20% (average of 15% Apr–Sep and 25% Oct–Mar). Group Housing Societies instead receive the Rs/unit ToD rebate (₹0.85/unit for FY27, shown as its % equivalent). ToD applicability for agriculture/residential should be verified against the full schedule; the ToD fields are editable.
    • Payback is multi-year and honest. Each year’s net saving is computed on that year’s tariffs (page 100-103 energy/wheeling, 106/108/110/112 CSS, InSTS transmission, and the stepped ToD rebate); the payback is the year cumulative saving recovers your equity (30% of project cost × your 26% share = 7.8% of cost, at ₹4.5 cr/MW by default, all editable). Because the solar-hours rebate deepens to 25% from FY 2027-28 while energy charges fall, the solar-hour saving shrinks over time — so a single good first year can be misleading. Beyond FY 2029-30 the net saving is assumed to continue at the FY 2029-30 level (held flat) to compute the payback period; if the FY 2029-30 net saving is not positive, the equity is reported as not recovered. The Financial year selector switches every charge and the snapshot to that year; the year-by-year table shows the full path. RE price is held flat and no panel degradation is applied, as you specified.
    • Discounted payback & the recommendation. Your equity is returned interest-free at the end of the PPA, so its real cost is the return you forgo on it — the discounted payback therefore values each year’s saving at your return on equity (15% p.a. by default, editable). The recommendation follows the savings trajectory: no saving in FY 2026-27 → do not enter a group-captive arrangement; savings that vanish in a later year → take a medium-term agreement only up to the last year with savings; savings through FY 2029-30 → a long-term PPA is safe today. Discounted payback is shown for the latter two.

      Method & formulae ▾

      For a slot with ToD factor t (−0.20 solar, 0 normal, +0.20 peak):

      Retail rate R = EC × (1 + t) + wheeling charge
      RE per delivered unit P = RE price ÷ [(1 − txn loss)(1 − wheeling loss)]
      OA cost O = P + txn charge + wheeling charge + CSS + addl + op ÷ units
      Saving per matched unit in slot = R − O

      Generation is matched to demand to maximise saving (peak first, then normal, then solar) within the banking limits — solar→solar only, normal→normal&solar, peak→all. Then:

      Stranded = deliverable generation − matched
      Stranded loss = surplus rate × stranded
      Net saving = Σ(matched × slot saving) − stranded loss

      Optimise maximises net saving over plant capacity, subject to stranded ≤ 10% of deliverable generation. Units: one unit = one kVAh; generation input is kWh per MW per month × capacity.
